## Goal
List every piece of information your website will show — before you write a single line of HTML.

## Why this step exists

Web developers call this a **content model**: a complete inventory of what a page needs to communicate. If you skip it, you end up with HTML that is missing things, or structured wrong, because you are making up the content while you type the code. Those are two different jobs. Do them separately.

## What a restaurant website typically shows

Think through each category below for *your* restaurant. You do not have to include all of them, but you should have a reason if you leave something out.

**Essential**
- Restaurant name — will appear in the page title and probably as a large heading
- Tagline or short description — what makes this place different
- Menu — the dishes, with names, short descriptions, and prices

**Important**
- Hours — when is the restaurant open?
- Location — address, neighbourhood, or how to find it
- Contact — phone number, email, or both

**Nice to have**
- About / story — who runs it, what is the history, why does it exist?
- Photos — of the food, the space, or the people

## Your job now

Make a written list of the actual content for *your* restaurant. Real words, real dish names, real invented hours — not placeholders.

You will be copying this text directly into your HTML in **C05**. The more complete your list now, the faster that step goes.

## Self-check

- You have a written content list for your specific restaurant.
- None of it says "Lorem ipsum," "dish name here," or "TBD."
- You have at least four menu items with names, descriptions, and prices.

When your list is complete, you are ready for **C03**.
